Advanced Topics

Learners are encouraged to select one or two tracks aligned with their specific theoretical interests or research goals rather than attempting to complete all tracks.

  • Metametaphysics, Grounding, and Metaontology — Investigates the semantic legitimacy, epistemology, and foundational methodology of metaphysical inquiry, focusing on metaontology, hyperintentional grounding, and naturalism.
  • Metaphysics of Science: Spacetime, Quantum Mechanics, and Laws — Explores how contemporary physics reshapes ontology through relativistic geometry, quantum non-separability, ontic structural realism, and dispositional essentialism.
  • Formal Ontology, Advanced Modality, and Mereology — Studies formal axiomatic systems of parthood, location topology, model-theoretic modal logic, counterpart theory, and metaphysical indeterminacy.
  • Social Ontology and Ameliorative Metaphysics — Analyzes the ontological reality of human-constructed entities, including institutions, social kinds (gender and race), conceptual engineering, and collective agency.
  • Metaphysics of Mind, Consciousness, and Emergence — Examines non-reductive physicalism, the causal exclusion problem, panpsychism, powers-based emergence, and biological personhood.

Metametaphysics, Grounding, and Metaontology

Investigates whether metaphysical disputes are substantive or merely verbal, establishing formal frameworks for ontological dependence and evaluating naturalistic constraints on first philosophy.

Metaontology, Realism, and Deflationism: Metametaphysics: New Essays on the Foundations of Ontology (David J. Chalmers, David Manley, & Ryan Wasserman, eds.) - The defining anthology evaluating whether ontological disputes are substantive or merely verbal, contrasting Quinean quantifier commitment with Carnapian deflationism and quantifier variance.

Metaphysical Grounding, Essence, and Fundamentality: Metaphysical Grounding: Understanding the Structure of Reality (Fabrice Correia & Benjamin Schnieder, eds.) - Features Kit Fine's seminal guide and systematic essays formalizing non-causal hierarchical priority, essentialist definitions, and hyperintentional dependence.

Naturalized Metaphysics and Scientific Methodology: The Tools of Metaphysics and the Metaphysics of Science (Ted Sider / Oxford John Locke Lectures) - A free university lecture series examining how formal metaphysical tools such as grounding, structuralism, and theoretical equivalence interact with and are constrained by empirical physics.

Metaphysics of Science: Spacetime, Quantum Mechanics, and Laws

Analyzes how the mathematical frameworks and empirical findings of fundamental physics inform metaphysical theories of space, time, matter, and nomological necessity.

Quantum Ontology and Wavefunction Realism: Quantum Mechanics and the Crisis of Scientific Realism (David Albert / Ergo) - A structured, open lecture series by Columbia philosopher of physics David Albert analyzing the measurement problem, non-locality, and competing ontologies including GRW, Everett, and Bohmian mechanics.

Spacetime Substantivalism, Relationalism, and the Hole Argument: Philosophy of Physics: Space and Time (Tim Maudlin) - A rigorous investigation into whether spacetime is an independent substance or a network of geometric relations, tracing the debate through Newtonian absolute space, special relativity, and general relativity's Hole Argument.

Ontic Structural Realism and Physical Invariance: The Structure of the World: Metaphysics and Representation (Steven French) - An advanced treatise arguing that modern physics reveals reality to consist fundamentally of mathematical and relational structures rather than self-subsistent objects.

Dispositional Essentialism and Laws of Nature: Nature's Metaphysics: Laws and Properties (Alexander Bird) - Formulates a comprehensive powers ontology where fundamental properties are essentially dispositional and laws of nature hold with metaphysical necessity rather than contingent regularity.

Formal Ontology, Advanced Modality, and Mereology

Employs formal logical and mathematical systems to model parthood, spacetime occupation, possible worlds semantics, and ontic indeterminacy.

Formal Modal Logic and Model Theory: Modal Logic (Stephen Yablo / MIT OpenCourseWare) - A full open university course with lecture notes and problem sets covering Kripke relational semantics, soundness, completeness, modal predicate logic, shifting domains, and counterpart theory.

Modal Realism and Counterpart Theory: On the Plurality of Worlds (David Lewis) - The masterwork of twentieth-century modal metaphysics defending genuine modal realism, concrete possible worlds, and the rejection of trans-world identity in favor of counterpart theory.

Formal Mereology, Parthood, and Location: Parts: A Study in Ontology (Peter Simons) - The definitive formal investigation of mereological axiomatizations, extensionality, atomism versus atomless gunk, and how material continuants occupy spacetime regions.

Vagueness and Metaphysical Indeterminacy: Vagueness (Timothy Williamson) - An authoritative text defending classical logic and epistemicism against fuzzy logic and degrees of truth, laying the foundation for modern debates over ontic vagueness and metaphysical indeterminacy.

Social Ontology and Ameliorative Metaphysics

Examines the constitution of social reality, the metaphysical status of social kinds, collective agency, and normative methods for re-engineering human concepts.

Foundations of Social Ontology and Institutional Reality: The Construction of Social Reality (John R. Searle) - The foundational monograph demonstrating how collective intentionality, status functions, and constitutive rules bring institutional facts, money, and legal borders into existence.

Social Kinds and the Metaphysics of Race and Gender: Resisting Reality: Social Construction and Social Critique (Sally Haslanger) - An influential collection applying analytic metaphysics to social structures, defining gender and race as hierarchically organized social kinds and introducing the ameliorative approach.

Conceptual Engineering and Semantic Revision: Fixing Language: An Essay on Conceptual Engineering (Herman Cappelen) - An advanced analysis of the epistemology and metasemantics of assessing, revising, and engineering concepts to overcome defective philosophical and political representations.

Group Agency and Collective Intentionality: Group Agency: The Possibility, Design, and Status of Corporate Agents (Christian List & Philip Pettit) - A systematic formal and metaphysical defense of the thesis that collective organizations and corporations can qualify as autonomous agents with emergent intentional states and moral responsibilities.

Metaphysics of Mind, Consciousness, and Emergence

Focuses on deep ontological dilemmas at the boundary of physics and philosophy of mind, evaluating mental downward causation, panpsychist fundamentality, and non-psychological accounts of human persons.

Mental Causation and the Causal Exclusion Problem: Mind in a Physical World: An Essay on the Mind-Body Problem and Mental Causation (Jaegwon Kim) - Formulates the classical supervenience and causal exclusion arguments, demonstrating the grave tension between non-reductive physicalism and the causal completeness of physics.

Panpsychism and Russellian Monism: Consciousness and Fundamental Reality (Philip Goff) - A comprehensive defense of panpsychism and Russellian monism, arguing that physical science reveals only the relational and mathematical structure of reality while consciousness supplies its intrinsic nature.

Metaphysical Emergence and Downward Causation: Metaphysical Emergence (Jessica M. Wilson) - Develops an exhaustive powers-based taxonomy of weak versus strong emergence, establishing rigorous criteria for how emergent phenomena depend upon yet remain efficacious with respect to their physical base.

Biological Animalism and the Nature of Persons: The Human Animal: Personal Identity Without Psychology (Eric T. Olson) - Challenges the dominant psychological continuity tradition by arguing that human beings are fundamentally biological organisms whose persistence conditions are independent of psychological continuity.
